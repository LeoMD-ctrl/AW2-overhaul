# Story

Living document. The user's premise first, then proposals and open
questions. Nothing here is final until the user says so.

## Premise (from the user, 2026-10-15)

- A **modern military faction is teleported by mistake** into the medieval
  fantasy world of Wargroove 2.
- The **anachronism** is what makes the battles interesting.
- **Former enemies become allies** against a **demonic faction**.

## Proposals linking the story to the systems (to discuss)

- **Stranded army = no supply chain.** Modern units hit hard but ammunition,
  fuel and reinforcements are scarce. This justifies:
  - persistent unit XP and CO proficiency (every veteran matters, the CO
    learns to fight in this world);
  - the engineer and its light camps (the faction must build its own bases);
  - roguelite perk choices as improvised adaptations.
- **Anachronism as gameplay:** range and firepower advantages for the modern
  faction, countered by magic, terrain, flying creatures and numbers.
  MANPADS and drones answer the world's flying units.
- **Former enemies to allies:** early missions against a local faction,
  then a forced alliance; the reputation / romance layer (axis 5) can track
  trust between the faction's officers and the local commanders.
- **Demonic faction:** reusing an existing faction's art for it would avoid
  a second full art set (Felheim, the undead faction, exists in the game
  files according to the modding guide's examples; its fit as "demonic" is
  to judge).

## Open questions

1. Who is the modern faction: a real-world-like army or a fictional nation?
   Its tone (serious, humorous, mixed)?
2. Why the teleportation: an accident on their side, a demonic ritual, the
   world's own magic? Is it linked to the demons from the start?
3. Who is the player's CO, and who are the key characters (a local rival,
   a love interest, a demon leader)?
4. Which local faction is the former enemy, which existing Wargroove
   characters appear, if any?
5. Ending: go home, stay, or a choice?
6. Length: number of missions (the earlier estimate assumed 5 to 8).

## Notes

- The "modern army sent to a fantasy world" setup is a known trope
  (e.g. the Japanese series *Gate*); the twist is what will make it ours.
